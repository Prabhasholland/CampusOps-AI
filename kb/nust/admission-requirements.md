Admission to an undergraduate programme requires a National Senior Certificate or equivalent, meeting both the university's general minimum entry requirements and the specific subject requirements of the programme applied for. Requirements differ meaningfully between faculties — engineering and health science programmes typically require stronger mathematics and science grades than programmes in humanities or commerce, so a general "do I qualify" question can't be answered without knowing the specific programme.

Applications are submitted online through the admissions portal and require certified copies of academic results, a certified copy of a national ID or passport, and the application fee. Uncertified or incomplete documents are one of the most common reasons an application is delayed rather than rejected outright — Admissions will usually request the missing document rather than closing the application, but the applicant needs to respond before a deadline to keep it active. An applicant who applied online can check their own application's progress by logging back into the same admissions portal with their reference number and pin — that self-service check is faster than waiting for a callback, so a caller asking how to check their status should be pointed there first.

Offers of admission are usually released in rounds rather than continuously, so a prospective student who applied recently may simply be waiting for the next round rather than facing a problem with their application. It's reasonable to explain this timing rather than implying something has gone wrong.

International applicants have an additional requirement: a certificate evaluating their qualification against the local grading system, obtained from the national qualifications authority before the application can be fully assessed.

Because admissions decisions depend on specific programme quotas and academic evaluation that a phone call cannot resolve on the spot, a prospective student calling about the status of their own application should be told that Admissions will follow up directly, with their application reference number noted down accurately so the right file gets pulled.
